# Pitch: Workflow Scout (draft 3, cut to three minutes)

About 455 spoken words. At the pace of your last read (3 minutes 40 for 570 words) this comes in at just under three minutes. Marcus speaks, Gerson drives. The earlier drafts are in the git history of this file.

## 0. The opening
Imagine choosing your next workflow automation without weeks of discovery calls. You drop a tool into your organisation for a few weeks, and it hands you a data based, validated, objective view of where your next automation sits, and what it is worth.

## 1. The problem
Every company hires people to do a job. In boutique firms especially, where everyone wears several hats, almost nobody ends up doing just the job they were hired for. The role description says client work. The week says copying numbers into slides.

make.com has made building an automation easy. So the hard question has moved from "can we automate this?" to "what should we automate next, and what is it worth?" Today that is answered by opinion, or by whoever shouts loudest.

## 2. The brief
make.com asked for an agent that finds automation use cases inside a company, with a daily check in, interview questions, prioritisation and human approval. A value engineer at make.com told us this is the problem she is trying to solve right now.

## 3. The demo (Gerson drives)
1. Brightline Advisory is a made up operations consultancy. Scout starts from three things every company already has for each role: the job description, its performance measures and its cost rate. From those it proposes how the role's time should be split. Tomas, the manager, adjusts one number and approves.
2. Elena is a Senior Client Consultant. Scout interviews her about her last working day. It already knows her calendar and her calls, so it only asks about what it cannot explain.
3. Elena sees her day: what her role expects against what happened. Nearly four hours went to manual status reporting, which is nowhere in her role. She corrects it and submits. Nothing reaches her manager until she does.
4. Tomas approves the day, then asks Scout to review three weeks of approved days. Top of the list: the weekly client status report, more than six hours a week across three people, priced from their cost rates.
5. He approves it. Five seconds later a real draft scenario is sitting in make.com, waiting for a person to finish it.

## 4. What is ours
We rank work by how far it is from what the person was hired to do, and by what it costs. The interviewer knows what it does not know, and asks only about that. And the numbers can be trusted, because the employee corrected them and the manager approved them.

## 5. The guard rail
This finds work to automate. It does not judge people. The employee corrects their own day before anyone else sees it. We attacked our own interviewer with Galtea, found real weaknesses, and fixed them.

## 6. The close
Next: reading role documents from the company's own store, and tracking whether each automation gave the hours back.

"Your automation pipeline, built for you, from evidence, aimed at giving people back the job you hired them for."
