# Pitch outline: Workflow Scout (draft 1)

Three minutes spoken, about 420 words. Marcus presents, Gerson drives the demo. Drafted Saturday 19 September 2026 from Marcus's own answers.

## 1. The problem (30 seconds)
Every company hires people to do a job. Almost nobody ends up doing the job they were hired for. The role description says client work. The week says copying numbers into slides.

Building an automation has never been easier. make.com has made that part simple. So the hard question has moved. It is no longer "can we automate this?" It is "what should we automate next, and how do we prove it was worth it?"

Today that question is answered by opinion, or by whoever shouts loudest.

## 2. The brief, in make.com's words (20 seconds)
make.com asked for an agent that finds use cases for automation inside a company: a daily voice check in, structured interview questions, continuous capture, prioritisation, and human approval.

We showed our plan to a value engineer at make.com. Her job is to work out which automations are worth most to an enterprise. She told us this is the problem she is trying to solve right now. Workflow Scout does that work from inside the company, with data.

## 3. The demo story (90 seconds, Gerson drives)
1. Tomas runs Client Delivery at Brightline Advisory. Scout has read the role descriptions from the company's own document store and proposes how each role's time should be split. Tomas adjusts one number and approves.
2. Elena is a Senior Client Consultant. This morning make.com emailed her a link. She clicks it and Scout interviews her about her last working day. It already knows her calendar and her calls, so it only asks about what it cannot explain: two empty hours on Friday morning, and a block called "Friday report send out".
3. Elena sees her day: what her role expects against what happened. Nearly four hours went to manual status reporting, which is nowhere in her role. She corrects one number and submits. Nothing reaches her manager until she does.
4. Tomas approves the day. Then he asks Scout to review three weeks of approved days for the team. Top of the list: the weekly client status report, about seven hours a week across three people.
5. He approves it. A real draft scenario appears in make.com.

## 4. What is ours (20 seconds)
- The yardstick is the job itself. We rank work by how far it is from what the person was hired to do, not only by how repetitive it is.
- An interviewer that knows what it does not know, and asks only about that.
- Suggestions built from days that the employee corrected and the manager approved, so the numbers can be trusted.

## 5. The guard rail (10 seconds)
This finds work to automate. It does not judge people. The employee sees and corrects their own day first, and says so on every screen.

## 6. Where it goes next (10 seconds)
More ways to see the work: email, messages, screen activity. And closing the loop: tracking whether each automation gave the hours back.
